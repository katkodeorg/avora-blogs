Ceremonial vs. Culinary Matcha: What's the Difference?

Ceremonial-grade and culinary-grade matcha differ in harvest, leaf quality, color, taste, and price. Here is how to choose the right grade for drinking, lattes, and baking.

“Ceremonial” and “culinary” are the two grades you’ll see most often when shopping for matcha. They aren’t official legal terms, but they describe a real and meaningful difference in quality and intended use.

Ceremonial-grade matcha

  • Harvest: first harvest (ichibancha), the youngest, sweetest leaves.
  • Leaves: young leaf tips, stems and veins removed, stone-ground.
  • Color: vibrant, living green.
  • Taste: smooth, full-bodied, naturally sweet, with rich umami and no harsh bitterness.
  • Best for: drinking on its own, whisked with just water.

This is the grade you want for a traditional bowl of matcha. Avora is ceremonial-grade, sourced from first-harvest leaves in Kyoto and Kagoshima.

Culinary-grade matcha

  • Harvest: later harvests, more mature leaves.
  • Color: duller, more olive or yellow-green.
  • Taste: stronger, more astringent and bitter.
  • Best for: lattes, smoothies, baking, and recipes where matcha is mixed with milk, sugar, or other ingredients.

The bitterness that makes culinary matcha unpleasant to drink straight is actually useful in a latte, where it cuts through milk and sweetener.

How to spot quality at a glance

Regardless of grade, good matcha is a bright, vivid green. A dull, brownish, or yellow tint is a sign of older leaves or oxidation. Learn more in our guide to why matcha tastes grassy.
