How to Choose a Matcha Brand (What Actually Matters)

Not sure which matcha to buy? A simple checklist for choosing a matcha brand: grade, harvest, origin, color, freshness, and price per serving.

Matcha ranges from cheap supermarket tins to expensive imported labels, and the packaging rarely tells you what matters. Here is a simple checklist for choosing a matcha brand, whichever one you go with.

1. Grade: ceremonial or culinary?

For drinking on its own or in a good latte, you want ceremonial grade. Culinary grade is cheaper per gram but more bitter, and it is built for baking and heavy recipes, not for sipping. See our full guide to ceremonial vs. culinary matcha.

2. Harvest: is it first-harvest?

The first harvest of the year (ichibancha) gives the sweetest, most nutrient-dense leaves. Brands that use first-harvest leaves, like Avora, taste smoother and less bitter.

3. Origin: where is it from?

Japan’s most respected matcha comes from regions like Kyoto (including the historic Uji area) and Kagoshima. A brand that names its region is usually prouder of its sourcing than one that just says “product of Japan.” Avora sources from Kyoto and Kagoshima.

4. Color: how green is it?

Vibrant, almost electric green means young, well-shaded, fresh leaves. Dull, yellowish, or brownish powder means lower grade or oxidation. Trust the green.

5. Freshness and packaging

Matcha fades once exposed to air and light, so look for a sealed, opaque tin and a brand that sells sizes you will finish while fresh. A 30g tin is a sensible amount for most people. (Here is how to store it.)

6. Price per serving, not price per tin

A big cheap tin is not a bargain if it tastes grassy and you never finish it. Work out the cost per serving instead. A 30g tin of Avora at 1,199 rupees makes about 15 bowls, which works out to roughly 80 rupees a serving, far less than a cafe drink. We break down the full math in is Avora matcha worth it?.

The short version

Look for ceremonial grade, first-harvest leaves, a named Japanese region, a vivid green color, fresh sealed packaging, and a fair price per serving. Avora was built to check every one of those boxes.
