How to Store Matcha So It Stays Fresh

Matcha is a fresh product that fades fast. Learn how to store matcha properly: away from air, light, heat, and moisture, so every bowl stays vibrant and smooth.

Matcha is a fresh product, not a pantry staple that lasts forever. Stored badly, even the best matcha turns dull, flat, and grassy within weeks. Stored well, it stays vibrant and smooth. Here is how to keep it at its best.

Matcha has four enemies

Matcha degrades when it meets any of these:

  • Air (oxygen): causes oxidation, which fades the color and flattens the flavor.
  • Light: breaks down chlorophyll and the delicate compounds that make matcha green.
  • Heat: speeds up every kind of degradation.
  • Moisture: clumps the powder and can spoil it.

Good storage is simply about keeping all four away.

The rules for storing matcha

  1. Keep it airtight. Reseal the tin tightly after every use, or transfer it to an airtight container. This is the single most important step.
  2. Keep it dark. Store it in a cupboard or an opaque tin, not on a sunny counter.
  3. Keep it cool. A cool, dry cupboard is ideal. Avoid spots near the stove, oven, or kettle.
  4. Keep it dry. Never let a wet spoon or steam near the powder. Use a dry spoon every time.

Avora’s matcha ships in a sealed tin designed to protect it. Just keep it closed tight and cool. If you want a fresh tin to start with, shop Avora ceremonial matcha.

Should you refrigerate matcha?

You can, but with one catch: condensation. If you keep matcha in the fridge or freezer, let the sealed tin come fully to room temperature before you open it, so moisture does not condense on the cold powder. For most people who finish a tin within a few weeks, a cool, dark cupboard is simpler and works perfectly.

How long does matcha last?

  • Unopened: several months to about a year, if kept sealed and cool.
  • Opened: best within four to six weeks, while the color and aroma are still bright.

Because opened matcha fades, buy it in amounts you will actually drink. If you go through it quickly, a fresh tin from Avora will always taste better than a large tin left half-used for months.

How to tell if matcha has gone stale

  • The color has shifted from vibrant green toward dull yellow or brown.
  • The fresh, sweet aroma has faded or smells hay-like.
  • The taste is flatter and more bitter than it used to be.

Stale matcha is not unsafe, it just is not enjoyable. If yours has turned, it may be time for a fresh tin of Avora.
