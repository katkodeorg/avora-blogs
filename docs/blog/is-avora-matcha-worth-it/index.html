Is Avora Matcha Worth It? Cafe Matcha vs. Making It at Home

A cafe matcha latte in India costs around 400 rupees. A 30g tin of Avora is 1,199 rupees and makes about 15 bowls. Here is the honest cost breakdown.

Good ceremonial matcha can feel like a splurge, until you do the math against what you are already spending. Here is an honest look at what matcha actually costs, and why making it at home with a tin of Avora is a genuine steal.

The cafe habit adds up fast

A matcha latte at a cafe in India usually costs somewhere around 400 rupees. Buy one a few times a week and you are spending 4,000 to 5,000 rupees a month on matcha alone, often oversweetened and made with unknown-grade powder.

What a tin of Avora actually costs per drink

A tin of Avora ceremonial matcha is 30g for 1,199 rupees. A single serving is 1 to 2 teaspoons, roughly 2 grams. So one tin makes about 15 bowls or lattes (more if you use a lighter scoop).

It is not just cheaper, it is better

Value is not only about price. With Avora you also get:

  • Ceremonial grade, made from first-harvest leaves from Kyoto and Kagoshima, not mystery powder.

The bottom line

If you drink matcha even once a week, a tin of Avora costs less than your cafe habit and tastes better. If you are new to making it at home, start with our step-by-step guide or the matcha latte recipe.
