Matcha and coffee are the two most popular ways to caffeinate, but they feel
completely different in the body. Here is how they compare, and how to decide
which one belongs in your morning.

## The quick answer

Coffee gives you a fast, strong spike of energy that fades with a crash. Matcha
gives you a gentler rise and a longer, steadier plateau, with far fewer
jitters. If you want raw intensity, coffee wins. If you want calm, sustained
focus, matcha wins. Many people, once they try a good ceremonial matcha like
[Avora](https://www.avoramatcha.com/product/ceremonial-matcha), switch for the
smoother ride.

## Caffeine: how much, and how it hits

A cup of coffee typically has more caffeine than a bowl of matcha. But the
number matters less than the delivery. Coffee's caffeine hits fast and leaves
fast. Matcha's caffeine is bound up with **L-theanine**, an amino acid that
slows its release and takes the edge off, so the energy arrives smoothly
instead of all at once.

## Jitters and the crash

The spike-and-crash of coffee is the most common complaint about it: the
racing heart, the mid-afternoon slump, the need for a second cup. Because
matcha releases its caffeine gradually, most people find it **does not spike
and does not crash**. That is the single biggest reason people make the switch.

## Focus

Coffee makes you alert. Matcha makes you calmly focused. The L-theanine plus
caffeine combination in matcha is well known for supporting concentration
without the wired feeling, which is why it is popular for long stretches of
deep work or study. If that sounds like your kind of energy,
[try Avora matcha](https://www.avoramatcha.com/product/ceremonial-matcha).

## Taste

This one is personal. Coffee is roasted, bitter, and bold. Good matcha is
vegetal, savory, and smooth, with a natural sweetness and rich umami. Bad
matcha is grassy and bitter, which is usually a sign of low grade or poor
technique, not the drink itself (here is
[why matcha tastes like grass](/blog/why-does-my-matcha-taste-like-grass/) and
how to fix it).
