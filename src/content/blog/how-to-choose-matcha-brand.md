Matcha ranges from cheap supermarket tins to expensive imported labels, and the
packaging rarely tells you what matters. Here is a simple checklist for choosing
a matcha brand, whichever one you go with.

## 1. Grade: ceremonial or culinary?

For drinking on its own or in a good latte, you want **ceremonial grade**.
Culinary grade is cheaper per gram but more bitter, and it is built for baking
and heavy recipes, not for sipping. See our full guide to
[ceremonial vs. culinary matcha](/blog/ceremonial-vs-culinary-matcha/).

## 2. Harvest: is it first-harvest?

The first harvest of the year (**ichibancha**) gives the sweetest, most
nutrient-dense leaves. Brands that use first-harvest leaves, like
[Avora](https://www.avoramatcha.com/product/ceremonial-matcha), taste smoother
and less bitter.

## 3. Origin: where is it from?

Japan's most respected matcha comes from regions like **Kyoto** (including the
historic Uji area) and **Kagoshima**. A brand that names its region is usually
prouder of its sourcing than one that just says "product of Japan." Avora
sources from Kyoto and Kagoshima.

## 4. Color: how green is it?

Vibrant, almost electric green means young, well-shaded, fresh leaves. Dull,
yellowish, or brownish powder means lower grade or oxidation. Trust the green.

## 5. Freshness and packaging

Matcha fades once exposed to air and light, so look for a **sealed, opaque tin**
and a brand that sells sizes you will finish while fresh. A 30g tin is a
sensible amount for most people. (Here is [how to store it](/blog/how-to-store-matcha/).)

## 6. Price per serving, not price per tin

A big cheap tin is not a bargain if it tastes grassy and you never finish it.
