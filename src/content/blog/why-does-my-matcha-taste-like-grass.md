If your matcha tastes like grass, pause. That harsh, bitter, lawn-clippings
flavor is not what good matcha is supposed to taste like — and it almost always
comes down to one of five fixable causes.

## 1. You're using culinary-grade matcha

The single biggest reason. Culinary-grade matcha is made from later-harvest,
more mature leaves and is designed to cut through milk and sugar in lattes and
baking. Drunk on its own, it tastes bitter and vegetal. For a smooth bowl,
you want **ceremonial-grade matcha** made from **first-harvest leaves** — like
[Avora](https://www.avoramatcha.com).

## 2. Your matcha isn't fresh

Matcha is a fresh product. Once opened and exposed to air, light, and heat, it
oxidizes — the vibrant green fades toward dull khaki and the flavor turns flat
and hay-like. Buy in small amounts, and finish an opened tin within 4–6 weeks.

## 3. Your water is too hot

Boiling water scalds matcha and pulls out bitter compounds. Use water at
**70–80°C (158–176°F)** — just below a boil. If you don't have a thermometer,
boil the kettle and let it sit for about 2 minutes first.

## 4. You're not whisking enough

Under-whisked matcha is clumpy and uneven, with concentrated bitter pockets.
Whisk briskly in a **"W" or "M" motion** for about 30 seconds until a fine,
even froth forms across the surface.

## 5. You're storing it wrong

Keep matcha in an **airtight tin, away from light and heat**. Once opened,
store it in the fridge and let it come to room temperature before opening to
avoid condensation.

## The bottom line

Grassy matcha is a signal, not a life sentence. Start with a genuine
ceremonial-grade matcha, keep it fresh and cool, use sub-boiling water, and
whisk properly — and you'll get the smooth, umami-rich bowl matcha is famous
for.
